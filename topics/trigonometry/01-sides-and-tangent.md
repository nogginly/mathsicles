## Stretch

1. One right triangle has opposite 6 cm and adjacent 8 cm. Another has opposite 9 cm and adjacent 12 cm. Do they have the same angle $\theta$? Explain how you know.
2. A right triangle has $\tan\theta = 0.5$ and an adjacent side of 10 cm. Find the opposite side, then the hypotenuse.
3. Using the triangle from the Practice page, calculate $\tan\varphi$. Compare it with $\tan\theta$. Will this happen in every right triangle? Why?

## Transfer: measuring a pyramid with a stick

According to legend, around 600 BC the Greek thinker Thales measured the height of the Great Pyramid without climbing it. He waited for a sunny day and used his shadow.

At the same moment, the sun's rays hit everything at the same angle. A stick 1 m tall cast a shadow 1.6 m long. The pyramid's shadow reached 232 m from the point directly below its top.

1. Sketch the two right triangles. For each, which length is the opposite and which is the adjacent?
2. Find $\tan$ of the sun's angle using the stick, then use it to find the pyramid's height.
3. Your turn: on a sunny day, measure your height, your shadow and the shadow of a tree or building. How tall is it?

*Hint for 1: the sun's angle sits where the shadow ends.*
