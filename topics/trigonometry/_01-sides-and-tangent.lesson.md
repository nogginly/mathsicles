## Lesson

Trigonometry links the **angles** of a right-angled triangle to the **lengths** of its sides. First, the sides need names.

![](figures/sides.svg){width=50%}

- **Hypotenuse**: the longest side, facing the right angle.
- **Opposite**: the side facing the angle $\theta$ (*theta*) we are looking from. It does not touch $\theta$.
- **Adjacent**: the other side touching $\theta$.

Opposite and adjacent depend on where you stand, like "left" and "right". Look from the other acute angle and they swap. The hypotenuse never moves.

**Steepness.** A ramp rising 1 m over 4 m has $\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} = \frac{1}{4} = 0.25$. A ramp rising 2 m over 8 m: $\frac{2}{8} = 0.25$. Same angle, same ratio, whatever the size.

This ratio is called the **tangent** of $\theta$:

$$\tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$$
